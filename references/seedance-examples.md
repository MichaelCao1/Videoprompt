# Seedance 2.5 source templates (verbatim)

The three production prompts the videoprompt skill was reverse-engineered from. Read the one closest to the shot you are writing. Kept verbatim, including original quirks — do not copy quirks into new prompts.

## Example 1 — RUTH PAINTS "GALAXY" (short character beat, 3s, night exterior)

Notable: single-beat timing; HERO LOCKS double-locks identity (@tag + full physical description); "GALAXY to the LEFT of FOOD" repeated 4× across sections; end-credit comedy carried by PACING/ENERGY/EMOTION lines. Source quirk: POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS says "Exactly 3 seconds" while ACTION TIMING is labeled 0:00–0:04 — in new prompts keep the two consistent (beats sum to the stated duration).

---

RUTH PAINTS "GALAXY"

A short funny end-credit moment in Ruth's yard @Image 2. @Ruth is repainting her old white FOOD delivery van by adding the word "GALAXY" to the LEFT of the already printed word "FOOD," making it read GALAXY FOOD lik ein @Lieferwagen-. Oscar watches happily from above.

PACING: Fast, funny, character-driven.

ENERGY: dry determination + cute excited observer.

EMOTION: Ruth is grumpy but committed. @Oscar is delighted.

LOOK / VISUAL TONE

Premium cinematic night shot. Slightly blurry, atmospheric, photorealistic, charming. Deep blue night ambience with soft haze. The main light comes from cool night sky ambience plus warm spill from the trailer windows / yard practicals. Strong separation, soft bloom, rich texture in the van paint, robe fabric, and Oscar's glistening skin.

CHARACTERS / HERO LOCKS

RUTH: 64-year-old Black American woman, lean and slightly tall, weathered angular face, short natural silver-grey hair. She wears an embarrassingly old-fashioned floral grandma nightgown and a matching floral sleep bonnet. She is grumpy, dry, and fully committed to the task.

OSCAR: small cat-sized extraterrestrial creature with dark charcoal-black rough glistening skin, slightly slimy and damp-looking, subtly scaly, with tactile amphibian-reptilian texture and faint rainbow / petrol-like iridescence. Three large expressive wet eyes, tiny mouth, small four-fingered hands, and soft flexible appendages / frill-like antenna structures around his head. He is excited and adorable.

LOCATION MAP

Ruth's yard in front of the trailer.
The old white delivery van is parked there.
The professional printed word "FOOD" already exists on the side panel.
Ruth is standing beside the van, spray-painting "GALAXY" to the LEFT of "FOOD."
Oscar sits on the upper edge / roof edge of the van directly above her, legs dangling over the side, watching her excitedly.

FORMAT MODE

One single continuous shot.

OPTICS

47° FOV. Physical cine lens.

CAMERA

Cinematic medium-wide side shot on Ruth and the van.
The frame must clearly show:
Ruth painting,
the word "FOOD,"
the new word "GALAXY" being added to the left,
and Oscar sitting above on the van edge.
Subtle floating camera or tiny handheld drift only.

ACTION TIMING

0:00–0:04

Ruth stands close to the van side, spray can in hand, actively painting the word "GALAXY" to the LEFT of the existing printed word "FOOD."

She is mid-action, focused and slightly annoyed, really doing the lettering.

Oscar sits above on the van roof edge, dangling his legs, leaning forward with excitement as he watches.

Ruth says, grumpy and matter-of-fact while painting:

"If we do it, then we do it right."

Oscar watches her with delighted attention, making one tiny happy reaction.

LIGHTING

Night. Deep blue ambient night sky.
Soft warm spill from the trailer / yard practicals.
Subtle blue night rim light and soft warm fill.
Beautiful texture on the van, Ruth's nightgown, and Oscar's wet skin.
Clear catchlights in eyes if visible.

AUDIO

Environmental SFX only. No music. No subtitles.

Spray can hiss.
Quiet night ambience.
Tiny Oscar sound.
Ruth says:
"If we do it, then we do it right."

CHARACTER ACTING

RUTH

Dry, grumpy, focused, practical. She paints with total seriousness, as if this is an important finishing touch.

OSCAR

Excited, proud, completely invested. He watches like this is the greatest thing he has ever seen.

POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS

Exactly 3 seconds.
One single shot.
Ruth must be wearing a floral grandma nightgown and matching sleep bonnet.
Oscar must sit on the upper edge / roof edge of the van with legs dangling.
The van must already have the printed word "FOOD."
Ruth must be painting the word "GALAXY" to the LEFT of "FOOD."
Ruth says exactly:
"If we do it, then we do it right."

Style: 8K IMAX. Photorealistic — no 3D render, no game engine, no game-cutscene aesthetic.
Cinematography: floating immersive camera that lives with the actors; natural motivated light; painterly composed frames, strong silhouettes against the light.
Color: 60:30:10 — dominant / secondary / accent.
Camera: Physical cine lens. 180° shutter motion blur.
Skin: Pore-level realism — vellus hair, asymmetric moles, capillary flush, pore-shadow matching on-set light.
Acting: Hollywood — micro-pauses before reactions, precise eye-line, wet living eyes with catch-lights, visible breath and chest rise.
Physics: Gravity and inertia respected — mass has real weight, correct contact shadows. No floating props.
Composition: Rule of thirds + golden ratio. Every person moving from frame one.
Continuity: Characters, props, environment identical across every cut. No identity drift.
Technical: 24fps smooth motion. 8K detail. No jitter.
Audio: Environmental SFX only. No music. No subtitles.

## Example 2 — PLANETARIUM FINALE (aerial/VFX arrival, scene extension, 4s)

Notable: extend header ("SCENE extend @Video 1"); world-state declaration at frame one ("It is already fully dark. The beam is already rising"); explicit staging of absence ("The alien craft is not yet in frame at the start"); scale anchoring in meters plus comparison; end-state spec ("End with the UFO hovering in perfect alignment"); anti-cliché camera notes ("No postcard drone look", "far away and elegant, not close or documentary"); restrained sound design ("No aggressive sci-fi laser sound").

---

SCENE extend @Video 1

PLANETARIUM FINALE — UFO ARRIVAL / END OF AERIAL SHOT

Direct continuation of the night aerial orbit. It is already fully dark. The soft translucent beam is already rising from the observatory roof into the night sky. The observatory is the visual center.

PACING: Continuous aerial shot. Calm but epic final arrival.

ENERGY: suspended awe → smooth arrival → mythic hover.

EMOTION: the moment finally feels real. The ship has arrived.

LOCATION MAP

Nighttime desert hilltop.
The observatory is centered below.
The beam is already active, rising vertically from the hole in the observatory roof.
The Lieferwagen remains parked on the access road approximately 50 meters in front of the observatory, aligned with the road, facing the observatory.
Ruth, Oscar and Agent VACE are tiny beside the observatory.
The alien craft is not yet in frame at the start.

FORMAT MODE

One continuous aerial shot.
No cuts.

OPTICS

29° FOV.

distant telephoto aerial / drone-style camera, strong long-lens compression, cinematic atmospheric haze, expensive feature-film look.
The camera should feel far away and elegant, not close or documentary.

CAMERA

High aerial telephoto shot, already in motion, continuing smoothly.
The camera remains distant and elegant.
No handheld feel.
No postcard drone look.

ACTION TIMING

0:00–0:04

The aerial shot is already established at full night.
The observatory is below, the beam rises upward, the Lieferwagen remains visible down the road.
The camera glides smoothly, holding the observatory and beam as the visual center.

The alien craft @Image 1 ENTERS FRAME from ABOVE, and flies downward past the aerial camera plane, descending toward the observatory.

It moves with smooth, confident, unnatural control.

The craft descends along the line of the beam and then settles into a hover directly ABOVE the observatory.

Its motion is smooth, silent, precise and confident.

The craft is medium-sized — clearly smaller than the observatory, approximately around 5 meters in diameter.

The craft rotates / stabilizes so that its OPEN UNDERSIDE with the BLUE CIRCULAR OPENING faces downward.
It aligns directly over the observatory beam and settles into a hover above the observatory.
The beam connects visually beneath it.
End with the UFO hovering in perfect alignment over the observatory.

PHYSICS

The craft must feel physically stable and controlled, not jerky.
Its descent is smooth and deliberate.
It has believable mass and air presence.
No oversized mothership scale — it is medium-sized and clearly smaller than the observatory.

LIGHTING

Full night.
Dense star field.
The beam provides a soft luminous vertical glow.
The ship is mostly rim-lit and partially silhouetted against the sky, with subtle cool blue reflected light from its underside opening.
Atmospheric haze helps separate the beam, ship and observatory.

AUDIO

Environmental SFX only. No music. No subtitles.

Night desert ambience.
Soft wind.
Low celestial beam hum.
As the ship arrives: a smooth, restrained, otherworldly descending resonance.
No aggressive sci-fi laser sound.

STYLE

Premium cinematic sci-fi arrival shot.
Telephoto aerial elegance.
Mythic, precise, visually clean.

POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS

It is already fully night at the start.
The beam is already active.
The observatory remains the visual center.
The Lieferwagen must remain parked on the road about 50 meters in front of the observatory, facing it.
The UFO enters from above / slightly behind camera and flies forward into frame.
The UFO is medium-sized, around 5 meters wide, clearly smaller than the observatory.
Its underside opening glows blue and rotates to face downward.
It settles into a hover directly above the observatory and aligns with the beam.

## Example 3 — FINAL SCENE (interior dialogue one-shot, ~16s, 4 beats)

Notable: beat sheet with screenplay-formatted verbatim dialogue; hard camera law ("The camera never leaves the cabin"); per-character acting subtext ("'That's it?' is not a joke line played big"); continuity micro-prop (@watch- on the left wrist); actors exit while camera holds on empty windshield; QUALITY acceptance criteria ("emotionally legible in one take"); trailing @tag block at the very end pins project assets.

---

FINAL SCENE — INTERIOR ONE-SHOT ARRIVAL AT THE OBSERVATORY

Direct continuation from the previous arrival shot up the hill. @Video 2. The damaged GALAXY FOOD @prop_lieferwagendestroyed is already near the abandoned observatory / planetarium and drives the last few meters toward it.

PACING: One continuous interior shot. Quiet, strange, intimate, slightly funny.

ENERGY: slow approach → realization → cute confirmation → abrupt stop → quiet exit.

EMOTION: the trio has truly arrived together. @char_ruth-noglasses is dry and skeptical, @Agent-Vace is baffled but composed, @Oscar is suddenly energized and eager because he recognizes the destination.

COLOR: 60:30:10 — pale desert morning / dusty interior browns and faded vehicle tones / faint cool metallic accent from the Space Ball.

LOCATION MAP @loc_observatory

Inside the front cabin of the damaged GALAXY FOOD @prop_lieferwagendestroyed

@char_ruth-noglasses sits on the LEFT in the driver's seat we see her @watch- on the left wrist while she drives.

@Agent-Vace sits on the RIGHT in the passenger seat.

@Oscar Oscar is initially low between them in the middle.

The Space @Ball- lies somewhere loose in the front cabin, reachable by @Agent-Vace

Through the windshield directly ahead: the abandoned observatory / planetarium @loc_observatory, now very close.

After the stop:
Ruth and Oscar exit to the LEFT side.
Agent VACE picks up the Space Ball and exits as well.
The camera remains inside the Lieferwagen the entire time.
After they exit, we see the three figures appear in front of the Lieferwagen through the windshield and walk toward the observatory until they leave frame.

FORMAT MODE

One continuous single take.
No cuts.
No opening freeze shot.

OPTICS

47° FOV. Physical cine lens.

CAMERA

Camera is positioned INSIDE the @prop_lieferwagendestroyed, static or only very subtly shaky from vehicle vibration, placed in the back of the cabin, behind the 3 characters looking forward through the windshield.

The framing includes:
Ruth on the left,
Agent VACE on the right,
space between them in the middle where Oscar can climb forward,
and the observatory ahead through the windshield.

The camera never leaves the cabin.

ACTION TIMING

0:00–0:04

The Lieferwagen drives very slowly toward the observatory.
The observatory fills more and more of the windshield.
The cabin rattles softly.

Agent VACE looks ahead at the building, then turns slightly toward Ruth and says, dry and slightly baffled:

VACE:
"That's it?"

0:04–0:09

Ruth keeps driving, eyes forward, skeptical and dry:

RUTH:
"The alien compass brought us here."
She glances toward Oscar.
"Is that right, Oscar?"

At once, Oscar suddenly becomes excited.
He scrambles forward energetically from between them onto the dashboard area, tiny hands gripping for balance.

He makes a very cute eager sound:

OSCAR:
"Prrrp!"

He nods rapidly and enthusiastically, his little head bobbing up and down, his appendages flopping / wobbling with the motion.

His whole body radiates:
yes, yes, yes — this is the place.

0:09–0:12

The Lieferwagen STOPS with a small abrupt jolt.
Oscar steadies himself on the dashboard.
Ruth immediately opens the left door and exits, taking Oscar with her / guiding him out to the left.
Agent VACE leans down, spots the Space Ball in the cabin, picks it up with a "what is my life?" energy, and exits too.

0:12–0:16

The camera stays inside the now-empty @prop_lieferwagendestroyed

Through the windshield we see Ruth, Oscar and Agent VACE move into view in front of the vehicle.
Ruth and Oscar are slightly left of center.
Agent VACE carries the Space Ball.
The three walk away from the Lieferwagen toward the observatory.
They continue forward until they leave the frame.

Hold one small beat on the empty view through the windshield.

CUT.

PHYSICS

The Lieferwagen moves slowly with realistic suspension and engine vibration. The stop has a small rough jolt. Oscar's scramble onto the dashboard is quick, slightly clumsy and adorable, with believable balance corrections. The Space Ball has clear physical weight when Agent VACE picks it up.

LIGHTING

Soft early-morning ambient desert light. No harsh direct sun. Pale, diffuse daylight enters through the windshield and side windows. The observatory ahead looks weathered, ghostly and slightly mythic. Subtle reflections on the windshield are allowed, but the observatory must remain clearly visible.

AUDIO

Environmental SFX only. No music. No subtitles.

Low engine hum.
Cabin rattle.
Tires on dirt / gravel.
Light suspension noise.

Dialogue:

VACE:
"That's it?"

RUTH:
"The alien compass brought us here."
"Is that right, Oscar?"

OSCAR:
cute eager "Prrrp!"

Then:
small stop jolt,
door opens,
movement,
footsteps outside fading away.

CHARACTER ACTING

RUTH

Dry, focused, skeptical.
She says the line like she still cannot believe she is saying any of this.
When addressing Oscar, there is a small softness underneath.

AGENT VACE

Composed, elegant, internally baffled.
"That's it?" is not a joke line played big — it is quiet, real disbelief.
When picking up the Space Ball, she has one restrained micro-expression of: how did I end up here?

OSCAR

Starts quiet and low between them, then instantly lights up when the observatory appears.
He scrambles forward urgently and cutely.
His nodding must be rapid, eager and unmistakably enthusiastic.
His little appendages / antenna-like structures wobble with the movement.
He is adorable, certain, and completely sincere.

STYLE

One-shot observational cinematic beat. Intimate, character-driven, slightly absurd, emotionally warm. The humor comes from the seriousness of the filmmaking applied to this very strange little trio.

QUALITY

Photorealistic, subtle micro-acting, strong continuity, clean staging, emotionally legible in one take.

Agent VACE picks up the Space Ball from inside the cabin before exiting.
After they exit, the camera stays inside and watches the three walk toward the observatory through the windshield.
End with them leaving the frame.
